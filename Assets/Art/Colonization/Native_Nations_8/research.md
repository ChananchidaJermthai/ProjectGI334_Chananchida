## 1. Wampanoag — Ousamequin (Massasoit) (c. 1623)

Region: Southern New England; present-day Massachusetts and Rhode Island.

Ousamequin was a Pokanoket sachem within the Wampanoag world. A 1623 account describes a broad wampum belt and a black wolfskin over his shoulder.

Game terrain: Broadleaf Forest, Prairie, Swamp. Supply: Food, Fur. Specialists: Farmers, Fur Trappers. Native rival: Narragansett. Aggressive: Low.

Use Narragansett as a scenario rival inspired by early seventeenth-century regional tensions; alliances also changed over time.

Flag: A rising sun above two coastal waves. A new landscape-based game emblem, not a historical national flag.

## 2. Powhatan — Wahunsenacawh (c. 1607)

Region: Tidewater Virginia and the Chesapeake coastal plain.

Wahunsenacawh was the paramount chief known to the English as Powhatan. He led a chiefdom of multiple communities when Jamestown was founded in 1607.

Game terrain: Broadleaf Forest, Prairie, Swamp. Supply: Food, Tobacco. Specialists: Farmers, Tobacco Planters. Native rival: Monacan. Aggressive: Medium.

Monacan is a documented regional rival. Food reflects maize agriculture; Tobacco is a simplified game category for Indigenous tobacco, not a European plantation uniform.

Flag: Maize above a branching river on rust red. New game emblem inspired by Tidewater agriculture and waterways.

## 3. Onondaga (Haudenosaunee) — Garakontie (c. 1660s)

Region: Central present-day New York; Great Lakes and northeastern woodlands.

Garakontie was an Onondaga chief and important seventeenth-century diplomat. He represents Onondaga here, not an absolute monarch of all Haudenosaunee nations.

Game terrain: Broadleaf Forest, Mixed Forest, Prairie. Supply: Food, Fur. Specialists: Farmers, Fur Trappers. Native rival: Wendat. Aggressive: Medium.

Wendat rivalry references seventeenth-century Haudenosaunee-Wendat conflicts; Garakontie's own career also included sustained peace negotiations.

Flag: One longhouse on a deep purple field. Original architectural emblem; not a reproduction of the Hiawatha Belt or a modern Haudenosaunee flag.

## 4. Wendat — Kondiaronk (c. 1690)

Region: Great Lakes region; Kondiaronk's community was associated with Michilimackinac.

Kondiaronk was a Tionontati-Wendat leader and diplomat. This c. 1690 depiction precedes his major role in the Great Peace of Montreal in 1701.

Game terrain: Mixed Forest, Broadleaf Forest, Prairie. Supply: Food, Fur. Specialists: Farmers, Fur Trappers. Native rival: Haudenosaunee. Aggressive: Low.

The faction uses the broad Wendat label with Kondiaronk's Tionontati background stated explicitly. Haudenosaunee is a historical scenario rival, not a permanent enemy.

Flag: A canoe and paired paddles on dark teal, referencing Great Lakes travel. Original game emblem.

## 5. Lenape — Tamanend (c. 1683)

Region: Delaware Valley; present-day Pennsylvania, New Jersey, Delaware and nearby areas.

Tamanend was a Lenape village sachem documented in late seventeenth-century Pennsylvania. He was not a king ruling all Lenape communities.

Game terrain: Broadleaf Forest, Mixed Forest, Prairie. Supply: Food, Fur. Specialists: Farmers, Fur Trappers. Native rival: Susquehannock. Aggressive: Low.

Susquehannock rivalry refers to earlier conflicts in the 1620s-1630s, not a claim that Tamanend fought them in 1683. Food and Fur match farming and winter hunting.

Flag: Two leaves flanking a river on forest green. Original landscape emblem, not a Lenape clan or tribal seal.

## 6. Narragansett — Miantonomi (c. 1640)

Region: Narragansett Bay and present-day Rhode Island.

Miantonomi was a Narragansett sachem active in the early seventeenth century. His recorded speech is a source for understanding Indigenous responses to colonial expansion.

Game terrain: Broadleaf Forest, Prairie, Swamp. Supply: Food, Tobacco. Specialists: Farmers, Tobacco Planters. Native rival: Pequot. Aggressive: Medium.

Pequot rivalry refers to the Pequot War period of 1636-1638, before this approximate portrait date. Tobacco Planters is a game abstraction for local cultivation.

Flag: A stylized shell above coastal waves on indigo. New game emblem referring to coastal resources and shell working.

## 7. Pequot — Sassacus (c. 1636)

Region: Coastal southeastern Connecticut and adjacent southern New England.

Sassacus was a Pequot sachem during the 1630s. The village design uses bark-covered dwellings and a palisade associated with fortified settlements of the period.

Game terrain: Broadleaf Forest, Prairie, Swamp. Supply: Food, Fur. Specialists: Farmers, Fur Trappers. Native rival: Mohegan. Aggressive: High.

Mohegan is a scenario rival in the context of the Pequot War. High aggression is solely an editable AI balance setting, not a characterization of Pequot people.

Flag: A palisade gateway on ochre with a red central panel. Original emblem inspired by settlement architecture.

## 8. Mexica (Aztec) — Moctezuma II (c. 1519)

Region: Basin of Mexico; Tenochtitlan and the wider Mexica tribute network.

Moctezuma II was the Mexica tlatoani at the arrival of Cortes in 1519. The city icon uses masonry buildings, twin temple shrines, canals and chinampa gardens.

Game terrain: Plain, Prairie, Swamp. Supply: Food, Cotton. Specialists: Farmers, Cotton Planters. Native rival: Tlaxcalteca. Aggressive: High.

Cotton includes exchange and tribute from warmer regions; it does not imply cotton plantations in the highland capital. Tlaxcalteca is a documented rival polity.

Flag: Stepped garden geometry over water on turquoise. Original landscape emblem; not Mexico's modern eagle flag or a claimed Aztec national flag.
